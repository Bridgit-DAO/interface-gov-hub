# A Statement on the Teilhard Test and the Overweb

The Overweb is exploring what it means to design digital infrastructure through the lens of the Teilhard Test.

Developed by Daveed Benjamin, Parham Pourdavood, and Dr. Michael Witmore, the Teilhard Test proposes a framework for evaluating whether the systems we build help humanity mature from adolescent power into adult responsibility.

As artificial intelligence, algorithmic systems, and networked technologies play a larger role in shaping human life, questions of reflection, coordination, accountability, and collective sensemaking take on greater significance. The Teilhard Test offers one approach to examining how technologies influence not only what we can do, but who we become together.

The Overweb is an effort to build infrastructure for a more visible, participatory, and trustworthy internet. In exploring the Teilhard Test as a design lens, we are interested in questions such as:

* How can a system increase reflexivity and awareness of consequences?
* How can it support coordination without coercion?
* How can it preserve human agency, diversity, and contextual meaning?
* How can responsibility grow alongside power?

The Teilhard Test remains an evolving proposal. Its concepts, assumptions, criteria, and applications deserve continued examination, critique, refinement, and experimentation. We see value in creating a space where those conversations can take place in public and where contributions can directly inform the evolution of the framework.

This site exists to support that process.

Researchers, technologists, philosophers, governance practitioners, educators, community leaders, and citizens are invited to engage with the work, contribute perspectives, challenge assumptions, propose improvements, and explore practical applications.

As our technologies evolve, so do the societies, institutions, and cultures that create and use them. Understanding that relationship may be one of the central challenges of our time.

## How to Participate

This site is designed as a living process of inquiry, refinement, and collective sensemaking.

You are invited to:

* Read the paper
* Watch the presentation
* Explore the slide deck
* Comment on specific passages
* Suggest edits and improvements
* Contribute references and critiques
* Join discussions and working groups
* Support the continued development of the framework

The goal is to deepen understanding, surface diverse perspectives, and strengthen collective judgment about the relationship between technological capability and human development.

As the Overweb evolves, we are exploring whether the Teilhard Test can help inform the design of systems, interfaces, governance processes, and collaborative environments that strengthen humanity's capacity for reflection, coordination, responsibility, and collective intelligence.

The question before us is how the next generation of digital infrastructure can help people think together, act together, and grow wiser together.
